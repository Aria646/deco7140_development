Ethics Analysis

Privacy Protection and Data Security

Privacy is one of the most critical ethical concerns. If a recipe website collects excessive personal data such as full names, phone numbers, or addresses, it increases the risk of privacy breaches. Likewise, if browsing histories and saved recipes are stored indefinitely without a deletion mechanism, users may face serious data security risks. To mitigate these risks, the website should follow the principle of data minimisation, collecting only necessary information, such as email addresses for subscription or login purposes. Phone numbers should be optional rather than mandatory. Local storage should be prioritised, while server-side data must have clear retention limits (e.g., 90 days), with a one-click delete option available to users. Data handling processes should remain transparent and compliant with both local and international privacy regulations.

Transparency and Traceability

Transparency directly affects user trust. If recipe sources, authorship, and publication dates are unclear, users may follow outdated or unreliable information. Similarly, if AI-generated or machine-translated content is presented without disclosure, users might assume it has been reviewed by humans, leading to potential misunderstandings. To address this, every recipe should clearly display the author, publication date, and data sources. Recommendation algorithms should provide explainability, such as “sorted by rating” or “sorted by popularity.” Any AI-generated or translated content must be explicitly labelled as such, with a disclaimer that it is “for reference only.” Critical information such as nutrition values or ingredient quantities should remain subject to human verification.

Accessibility and Fairness

Accessibility and fairness are essential to ensuring inclusivity. Without features such as alt text, video captions, or high-contrast display options, visually or hearing-impaired users may be excluded. In addition, recommendation algorithms that overly prioritise popular or high-calorie recipes risk reinforcing unhealthy dietary habits while neglecting vegetarian, low-sugar, or allergy-sensitive users. The website should fully implement WCAG 2.2 AA standards by supporting screen readers, captions, large-text modes, and high-contrast themes. Recommendation systems should balance the promotion of healthy meals, diverse cultural cuisines, and dietary requirements to avoid bias and provide fair access to all user groups.

Accuracy and Compliance

Accuracy and compliance are central to maintaining credibility. Some recipe websites use exaggerated claims such as “zero fat” or “100% healthy,” which can mislead users and potentially constitute false advertising. Furthermore, user-uploaded images or videos without proper copyright attribution may result in intellectual property disputes. To prevent these risks, the website should avoid absolute or misleading statements. All nutritional information must be accompanied by clear references and calculation methods. For user-generated content, the platform should require contributors to specify whether their materials are original, licensed, or provided by partners, with a copyright review process in place to prevent infringement.

Safety and Commercial Transparency

Food safety and commercial transparency are essential to user trust. If recipes fail to provide allergen warnings, users with severe allergies may face health emergencies. At the same time, if advertisements or sponsored content are integrated into natural recommendations without being clearly labelled, this undermines fairness and erodes trust. To address these concerns, allergen information (e.g., peanuts, dairy, seafood) must be prominently displayed in recipe ingredient lists, alongside guidance or emergency hotline information. Commercially, all advertising and sponsored recommendations should be explicitly labelled as “advertisement” or “sponsored content” to ensure transparency and protect the integrity of natural recommendations.
