Ethics Response

Ethical Summary

The developer designed the subscription plug-in for a recipe-sharing website to enhance user engagement through community updates and personalised content. While the feature improves interactivity, it also introduces ethical challenges related to data minimisation, privacy, transparency, and user autonomy.

In the ethical review stage, the developer identified that collecting information beyond what is necessary for subscription — such as names, demographics, or personal preferences — would violate the public interest principle of the ACS Code of Ethics. To address this, only essential data, specifically users' email addresses, are collected. This ensures compliance with data minimisation and reduces privacy risks.

To promote informed consent, the developer implemented a double opt-in mechanism. Users must confirm their subscription via email before activation, and any unverified data are automatically deleted after 90 days. This guarantees that participation is both voluntary and verifiable.

Transparency and honesty are also prioritised. The interface clearly describes the email frequency and content — for example, "Weekly Digest: 3-5 recipes, one meal plan, and seasonal tips". Users can unsubscribe or modify their preferences at any time through visible links placed at the top of every email.

The design additionally reflects respect for diversity and inclusion by allowing users to choose dietary tags such as vegetarian, halal, or nut-free, and by avoiding culturally biased or unverifiable health claims. These decisions align with ACS principles of respect for diversity, honesty, and public welfare.

Overall, the ethical foundation of this plug-in lies in privacy protection, informed consent, accessibility, and cultural sensitivity. The implementation demonstrates how individual developers can integrate professional ethics into interface design, ensuring both usability and social responsibility.

Ethical Decision and Reasoning

The developer's ethical reasoning follows professional and philosophical frameworks, including Consequentialism, Deontological ethics, and Virtue ethics, combined with the ACS Code of Professional Conduct. These frameworks guided decision-making throughout the design and development process.

Application of Ethical Frameworks

  • ✔ Consequentialism: The developer assessed possible outcomes of design choices and prioritised those that maximise user benefit and minimise harm. Over-collection of personal data could lead to privacy breaches and reputational loss, whereas a minimal-data model protects users and strengthens trust.
  • ✔ Deontological Ethics: From a duty-based perspective, the developer recognised an ethical obligation to protect user privacy even when stricter data control required extra implementation effort. The adoption of double opt-in verification, visible unsubscribe links, and secure deletion reflects a commitment to moral duty over convenience.
  • ✔ Virtue Ethics: Throughout the process, the developer acted with integrity and empathy, ensuring clear communication, inclusive tone, and accessibility for all user groups. These actions reflect professional virtues expected of ethical computing practice.

Balancing Technical and Ethical Priorities

Rather than treating accessibility and privacy as compliance add-ons, the developer integrated them as core design priorities. Elements such as <fieldset< grouping for related inputs, visible keyboard focus indicators, and compliant colour contrast ratios ensure that users relying on screen readers or keyboard navigation have equal access. These actions fulfil WCAG 2.2 success criteria, including 1.3.1 (Info and Relationships), 2.4.7 (Focus Visible), and 1.4.3 (Contrast Minimum).

By addressing both usability and inclusivity at the technical level, the developer ensured that ethical intentions are embedded within functional design decisions.

Reflection on Ethical Practice

The developer recognises that small technical decisions — such as how consent is requested or how unsubscribe options are presented — can significantly affect user trust and rights. Embedding ethical thinking throughout the process transformed the project from a purely functional task into a reflection of digital responsibility.

Ultimately, this ethical approach illustrates that individual designers and developers can uphold fairness, accessibility, and autonomy while achieving professional and creative goals. By aligning design actions with ACS principles and moral reasoning, the subscription plug-in supports sustainable community engagement built on respect and trust.

Draft Response Letter to the Client

Subject: Trust-First Subscription Proposal for CCI Recipes

Dear Client,

Thank you for inviting us to explore the subscription plug-in for the CCI Recipes platform. We truly appreciate your commitment to creating a warm, community-based space that connects people through food and shared experiences. Our goal is to help you achieve this vision in a way that strengthens user trust while ensuring the platform remains ethically sound, transparent, and inclusive.

During our review of the proposed subscription feature, we identified several ethical challenges that often arise in similar systems. These include the over-collection of user data, limited transparency about how that data will be used, and insufficient user control over their preferences or consent.

For example, collecting details beyond what is necessary for a subscription—such as names, locations, or personal interests—may expose users to privacy risks or unwanted profiling. Similarly, unclear descriptions of subscription frequency or hidden unsubscribe links could lead users to feel deceived or locked in. Such issues conflict with key principles of the ACS Code of Professional Conduct, particularly those concerning public interest, honesty, privacy, and respect for diversity.

As the developer, I recognise that these risks can erode user confidence and damage the credibility of an otherwise valuable feature. Therefore, our approach focuses on re-establishing transparency and empowering users to make informed, voluntary choices.

To balance usability with ethical responsibility, we recommend implementing a model that embodies consent, clarity, and accessibility:

  • Double Opt-In (Reliable Consent): Users subscribe with their email and confirm via a one-click verification link. Unverified or inactive emails are automatically deleted after 90 days, ensuring participation is both voluntary and accountable.
  • Data Minimisation: Only essential information (email) is collected. Optional dietary tags (e.g., Vegetarian, Halal, Nut-Free) are available for those who wish to personalise content but can be removed at any time.
  • Transparency in Frequency and Purpose: Subscription pages clearly state what users can expect — for instance: "Weekly Digest: 3-5 recipes, one meal plan, and seasonal cooking tips". Users can also choose bi-weekly delivery or pause updates for 30 days.
  • User Control and Accessibility: Each email includes a visible “Unsubscribe / Manage Preferences” link at the top, and these options are mirrored in account settings. The interface follows WCAG 2.2 standards to support screen readers, keyboard navigation, and inclusive design.
  • Cultural Sensitivity and Respect: Content avoids unverified health claims or culturally biased messages. Users can opt for dietary-specific themes such as Vegetarian, Vegan, or Low-sodium, reflecting respect for diverse beliefs and lifestyles.

This design ensures that ethical principles are not treated as afterthoughts, but as integral parts of user experience and system architecture.

From a consequentialist perspective, this model reduces risks and maximises user benefit by preventing misuse of data and building lasting trust. From a deontological view, it fulfils the developer's duty to protect privacy and promote user autonomy, even when extra implementation effort is required. In practice, these measures also align with virtue ethics, reflecting honesty, empathy, and professional responsibility.

By integrating ethical reasoning directly into technical design, the project goes beyond compliance — it demonstrates how a developer can create a socially responsible digital product that genuinely respects its users.

We recommend beginning with a two-week pilot (10% of users) to evaluate opt-in rates, feedback, and unsubscribe patterns before full rollout. Our team is ready to assist with technical setup, compliance documentation, and accessibility testing to ensure a smooth and ethical implementation.

Thank you again for entrusting us with this opportunity. We look forward to helping you deliver a subscription feature that not only enhances user engagement but also sets a standard for ethical, transparent, and user-first design.

Warm regards,
