GenAI MT Acknowledgement

GenAI and Machine Translation Usage Statement

Tools Used

ChatGPT: This tool was mainly used to generate JavaScript examples, explain CSS syntax, debug navigation issues, and provide suggestions for layout optimisation and responsive design. It was also used to improve the fluency, coherence, and professionalism of my writing, ensuring that all documentation was clear and formal. In addition, I used it to refine grammar, logic, and tone to maintain high readability and academic quality across the project.

Prompting Strategy and Interaction Process

Throughout the project, I adopted a multi-step prompting strategy and improved it through continuous practice.

For example, when I wanted to build a Community Form and connect it with an API using JavaScript, I shared my existing HTML and CSS files with ChatGPT and asked:

"How can I use JavaScript to connect with an API? Please give me some JavaScript examples and explain the code in detail."

Sometimes, ChaGPT's answers were incorrect or didn't work when tested. When this happened, I carefully reviewed the code comments and tried small modifications. If the problem still wasn't solved, I would follow up with another question:

“Why does the login keep showing an error no matter what I input? The JavaScript doesn't seem to work. Please explain the cause and provide an example.”

Eventually, after spending some time without success, I asked my classmates for help and found out the issue was caused by a broken API. This experience taught me that when a problem seems unsolvable on my own, it's better to seek help early instead of wasting time trying to fix it with AI.

I also frequently used ChatGPT to clarify issues related to accessibility tools, for example:

  • "What does this error actually mean?"
  • "Why is it flagging a problem unrelated to my file?"
  • "How can I locate this issue in VS Code?"

In these cases, ChatGPT's explanations were often helpful. As the project progressed, I became more skilled at using the tools and no longer relied on AI excessively.

Critical Evaluation of AI Output

During my interactions with ChatGPT, I noticed that its responses sometimes appeared correct but contained semantic or factual inaccuracies. For instance, when I asked about WCAG 2.2 standards, ChatGPT said, “The contrast ratio between colour and text should be 4.5:1.”

At first glance, this seemed right, but when I checked the official WCAG documentation, I found that this statement was misleading — the standard applies to text and image contrast, not simply colour and text. This showed that AI often reassembles fragments of information from various sources without fully understanding the context or logic behind them.

A similar issue occurred with text summaries. ChatGPT's writing was grammatically correct and well-structured, but it often overgeneralised, omitted key details, or fabricated small facts. Even when I provided the full document, it sometimes ignored the context and produced summaries that looked polished but were inaccurate. For this reason, I treated AI output only as a reference for ideas, not as final content.

In visual design, ChatGPT's suggestions also lacked consistency and aesthetic balance, and its understanding of WCAG 2.2 accessibility was incomplete (e.g., incorrect application of contrast ratios). To ensure high quality, I cross-checked its outputs with professional design platforms and templates, which helped me maintain clarity, readability, and inclusivity for users with low vision. Through this process, I realised that AI still has clear limitations in visual perception and aesthetic judgement and should be used as a supportive tool, not a substitute for human design reasoning.

Challenges and Coping Strategies

The main challenge I faced was ChatGPT's inconsistent understanding of context. In long multi-turn conversations, it often focused only on the latest message while forgetting earlier information, leading to contradictions or "semantic drift". For example, when discussing WCAG 2.2, it first correctly mentioned the 4.5:1 contrast ratio but later applied it incorrectly to colour matching rather than image-text contrast.

To address this, I used two main strategies:

Step-by-step questioning and context anchoring - I broke complex queries into smaller, clearly defined sub-questions. For each stage, I restated the context and confirmed shared understanding. For example, I first checked whether ChatGPT “understood what WCAG is” before asking it to explain specific criteria. When necessary, I pasted earlier correct outputs back into the chat to keep the discussion consistent.

Cross-verification with authoritative sources - Whenever I dealt with technical standards, definitions, or data, I verified ChatGPT's information against official documents such as W3C, MDN, or ACM/ACS ethical guidelines. This dual-checking approach prevented misinformation and helped me build a more systematic verification process.

Through these methods, I managed to retain ChatGPT's creative benefits while reducing the risks caused by semantic drift.This experience made me realise that human oversight and critical evaluation are essential to ensure both accuracy and academic integrity when working with AI.

Impact on Productivity and Creativity

Despite challenges such as context inconsistency and occasional errors, ChatGPT greatly improved my productivity and creative thinking.

In the early stages, when I was still learning CSS and front-end structure, ChatGPT's examples and explanations helped me quickly understand style reuse, responsive layout, and modular design. By interacting repeatedly, I learned how to break complex styles into maintainable components and gained a deeper understanding of what makes code sustainable. This acted as an instant coaching mechanism, shortening the learning curve from beginner to independent developer.

ChatGPT also saved significant time on repetitive tasks, such as debugging navigation bars, adjusting card spacing, and aligning layouts. This efficiency allowed me to focus more on accessibility and user experience improvements. For instance, when testing keyboard navigation and focus states, I combined AI's guidance with manual checks to meet WCAG 2.2 requirements for visible focus and contrast.

As the project evolved, my approach shifted from "relying on AI for answers" to "using AI for exploration and reasoning." I started asking more critical questions, such as "Why is this solution better?" or "Will this logic work consistently across browsers?" This deeper reflection moved me beyond surface-level coding toward design-level reasoning.

More importantly, AI's instant feedback encouraged an open and creative mindset. Instead of passively accepting its responses, I learned to question, compare, and reconstruct ideas, which led to more independent and innovative problem-solving.

Overall, ChatGPT served as both a productivity booster and a catalyst for reflective learning. It helped me integrate technical implementation, accessibility awareness, and ethical thinking, achieving a balance between speed, quality, and responsibility. Through this process, I transitioned from a mere tool user to a critical and creative designer.
